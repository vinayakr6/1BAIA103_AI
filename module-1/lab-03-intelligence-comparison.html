1. Theory: Intelligence in AI

Intelligence can be discussed through capabilities such as perceiving information, learning from experience, reasoning, solving problems, making decisions and adapting behaviour toward goals. It is not one single ability; different systems can demonstrate different combinations and levels of these capabilities.

Perception

Receiving and interpreting information from the environment.

Learning

Improving performance using information or experience.

Reasoning

Using information to derive conclusions or select actions.

Problem Solving

Finding a way from a given situation to a desired goal.

Decision Making

Selecting among alternatives using goals, information and constraints.

Adaptation

Changing behaviour as circumstances or information change.

Key idea: Intelligence should be considered in relation to capabilities, tasks, goals and context.

2. Human Intelligence vs Machine Intelligence

Human and machine intelligence are better compared by specifying the task and characteristics being considered. A machine can be extremely capable on a narrow, well-defined task without possessing the broad range of abilities associated with human intelligence.

DimensionHuman IntelligenceMachine Intelligence
GeneralityBroad ability across varied tasks and contexts.Often optimized for particular tasks or operating conditions.
LearningCan learn from experience and relatively few examples in many situations.Can learn from data and training procedures; performance depends on data, model and objective.
SpeedLimited by biological processing.Can process large amounts of data rapidly with suitable resources.
ConsistencyCan vary with attention, fatigue, emotion and context.Can repeat procedures consistently under similar conditions.
ContextCan combine social, physical and common-sense experience.Depends on the information, data and interfaces available to it.

8. Reflection

  1. What do you understand by intelligence in AI?
  2. List four components of intelligent behaviour and explain them.
  3. Give two situations where machine intelligence is especially useful.
  4. Give two situations where human intelligence has an important advantage.
  5. Why is it misleading to call machines simply “more” or “less” intelligent than humans without specifying a task?
Conclusion: Intelligence is multidimensional. Machines may be highly capable in selected tasks while human intelligence remains broad and adaptable across many contexts.
