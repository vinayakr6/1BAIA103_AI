1. Theory: History of AI

The supplied Module 1 material highlights the development of AI from early mathematical models and computational ideas to modern AI applications. The source specifically lists these milestones:

1943
Artificial neuron model

Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts proposed a mathematical model that helped lay foundations for artificial neural networks.

1950
Turing Test

Alan Turing introduced a behavioural test for machine responses that could be indistinguishable from human responses.

1956
Dartmouth Conference

John McCarthy coined the term Artificial Intelligence during the Dartmouth conference.

1997
Deep Blue

IBM Deep Blue defeated chess champion Garry Kasparov, demonstrating strategic game-playing capability.

2011
Siri

Apple introduced Siri, bringing AI-based natural-language interaction to everyday personal assistance.

Remember: history tells us when the field and important systems developed; AI types describe the capabilities or functionality of systems.

2. Theory: Types of AI

The supplied course material includes Weak AI, Strong AI, Reactive Machines, Limited Memory, Theory of Mind and Self-Awareness.

01 · CAPABILITY

Weak AI

Designed to perform a specific type of task. It operates within its intended problem area.

Key idea: task-specific intelligence.
02 · CAPABILITY

Strong AI

Also associated in the source with AGI. It aims to mimic human thinking and perform tasks it has not been specifically trained for.

Source status: future / not yet a reality.
03 · FUNCTIONALITY

Reactive Machines

Respond to current stimuli. The source uses IBM Deep Blue as an example.

Key idea: current situation → response.
04 · FUNCTIONALITY

Limited Memory

Retain data for a short period and use it for decisions. Autonomous vehicles and AlphaGo are discussed examples.

Key idea: recent information → decision.
05 · FUNCTIONALITY

Theory of Mind

A proposed form of AI that would represent thoughts, emotions and memories and use understanding of feelings in decisions.

Source status: theoretical.
06 · FUNCTIONALITY

Self-Awareness

A proposed stage involving human-level consciousness and understanding of the machine's own existence.

Source status: does not yet exist.
