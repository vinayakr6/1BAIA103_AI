Depth-Limited & Iterative Deepening DFS

Understand how a depth limit prevents DFS from going beyond a chosen depth, and how Iterative Deepening Depth-First Search (IDDFS) repeatedly increases that limit until the goal is found.

1. Theory: Depth-Limited Search

Depth-Limited Search (DLS) is a depth-first search with a fixed depth limit ℓ. Nodes at the limit are not expanded further. This prevents a DFS procedure from continuing indefinitely down a deep path.

Depth 0

Only the initial node is considered.

Depth 1

The initial node and its immediate successors may be explored.

Depth ℓ

Nodes at depth ℓ may be tested for the goal but are not expanded beyond the limit.

Cutoff ≠ failure: A cutoff means the search reached its depth limit before determining whether a deeper solution exists. Failure means the search has exhausted the relevant search space without finding a solution.

2. Iterative Deepening DFS (IDDFS)

IDDFS combines the depth-first memory behaviour of DLS with repeated increasing depth limits. It first searches with limit 0, then limit 1, then limit 2, and continues until a goal is found.

6. Why Use IDDFS?

Advantages

  • Uses much less memory than BFS in typical tree-search settings.
  • Can find a shallow solution without committing permanently to one deep branch.
  • The increasing limit makes the search systematic.

Trade-off

  • Nodes near the top can be generated more than once.
  • Choosing a single DLS limit is difficult when the solution depth is unknown.
  • Performance depends on the search-space structure and repeated work.
